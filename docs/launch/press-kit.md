### 200 words

Piblo is a small pocket device for the things people lose in a busy day: a note, an idea, the bins on Thursday. Hold a key, say it, put it away. The aim is less screen time and more time for everything else.

It is designed in the UK by one maker and built in the open. The firmware, the desktop software and the website are on GitHub under the MIT licence.

The device is an off-the-shelf M5Stack Cardputer ADV, with a keyboard, a small screen, a microphone and a speaker. It connects over Wi-Fi to Piblo Core, software on a Windows PC. The PC does the heavy work. The device is the controls. Piblo does not replace a phone and does not run AI models itself.

What is tested today: Core answers questions using free cloud AI and switches provider when one fails, the device stays in sync with Core over Wi-Fi, and firmware updates over Wi-Fi. Hold-to-talk voice, voice capture and a yes-or-no approval for PC actions are in testing.

Piblo is a prototype. There is no price, no release date and no Kickstarter date. The printed case, and ideas such as a hinged or sliding version, are concepts and are labelled that way.

Less screen. More life. Join the list at piblo.co.uk.
