What makes a Nod

What's in a Nod.

  1. Concept

    The case

    A snap-on front and back in orange, cream or mint, made for a home 3D printer. Not printed or fit-tested yet.

  2. Real hardware

    The device

    An off-the-shelf Cardputer ADV from M5Stack, with 56 keys, a 1.14-inch screen and a microphone. We don't make it, and it isn't made in Britain.

  3. In testing

    The software

    A typed-note feature is in the firmware. Device test pending.

Nothing is for sale yet: no prices, preorders or delivery dates.

Design concepts

Future directions for Nod.

Three ways the hardware could evolve, all drawn around the real Cardputer ADV. None are prototyped, costed or scheduled.

Nod Flip

A hinged screen that opens to reveal a full keyboard. Closed it fits in a pocket; open it becomes a mini laptop for longer sessions.

Design concept

Nod Slide

A sliding keyboard mechanism. Push up to reveal the keys for typing; slide back for a compact screen-only view.

Design concept

Nod Snap

A touchscreen module that snaps onto the back for visual tasks, plus modular add-ons. The core device stays the same; the shell adapts.

Design concept

What do I need to try it?

Development uses a Cardputer ADV and a connected Windows PC. Some optional online services may cost extra. A person always approves anything important, such as starting a print.

Is there a Kickstarter date or price?

Not confirmed. A reliable prototype, safety testing and real manufacturing quotes come first. Join the list below for honest updates.
