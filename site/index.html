06Questions

Straight answers

What works today?

Piblo is a development prototype. Earlier tests demonstrated a Wi-Fi connection to a Windows computer and a saved spoken note. Voice capture reliability and response times still need work. The feature screens here are illustrations, and the features are still in testing.

What is Piblo Flow?

Flow is voice typing for your connected computer, with tools to clean up dictated text. It is still in testing and needs a computer and speech service. It is not a finished standalone device feature.

Does it need a computer?

Today, yes: the prototype pairs with a Windows computer. The plan is that a phone will be enough for everyday reminders and notes, with a computer giving you the best voice. Android comes next, then Linux, macOS and iPhone.

Does it work offline?

It needs your home or office Wi-Fi to reach your phone or computer. Reminders and notes are designed to stay on your own devices, without the internet. Spoken answers need a speech or AI service, which is usually online unless you run one yourself.

Can an AI do something without asking me?

Reading, summarising and reminding just happen. Anything that sends, books or posts is designed to wait for you: Piblo will show who, what and how much on its own screen, then wait for your yes. That part is still in testing. Payments and deletions will stay off unless you switch them on.

How is it different from my phone?

It does a few things and nothing else. No apps, no feeds, no notifications you didn’t ask for. It shows one thing at a time and you answer with a real key, so you can deal with it and get back to your day.

How much will it cost?

Price to be announced. We’ll share it with the launch list first. Shipping will be extra.

When does the Kickstarter start, and when would it ship?

There is no date yet. We’ll only set one when the prototype is proven and the costs are checked, and backers will get an honest ship estimate based on real manufacturing progress.
