What makes a Nod

What's in a Nod.

A real pocket computer, a case you can print, and software you can read.

  1. Concept

    The case

    A snap-on front and back in orange, cream or mint, made for a home 3D printer. Not printed or fit-tested yet.

  2. Real hardware

    The device

    An off-the-shelf Cardputer ADV from M5Stack, with 56 keys, a 1.14-inch screen and a microphone. We don't make it, and it isn't made in Britain.

  3. In testing

    The software

    A typed-note feature is in the firmware. Device test pending.

Nothing is for sale yet. There are no prices, preorders or delivery dates.

Designs under investigation

Future directions for Nod.

A concept is a question, not a promise. Each of these explores a different way Nod's hardware could evolve — all built around the Cardputer ADV's real dimensions. None have been prototyped, costed or scheduled.

Nod Flip

A hinged screen that opens to reveal a full keyboard. Closed it fits in a pocket; open it becomes a mini laptop for longer sessions.

Design concept

Nod Slide

A sliding keyboard mechanism. Push up to reveal the keys for typing; slide back for a compact screen-only view.

Design concept

Nod Snap

A touchscreen module that snaps onto the back for visual tasks, plus modular add-ons. The core device stays the same; the shell adapts.

Design concept

These are exploratory hardware concepts for the Nod project — currently focused on the Cardputer ADV-based software prototype. No commitment has been made to manufacture any specific design.

Straight answers

Does Nod already analyse my saved Instagram Reels?

No. Video Vault is planned. We still need to prove importing accessible video, analysing both sound and visuals, and returning accurate source-backed answers. Instagram's saved-data export may provide links but not the video files.

Can Nod already slice and start a 3D print?

No. Reel-to-Print is a development concept. The proposed system would find a legitimate STL/3MF, verify its licence and suitability, slice on the connected computer and upload the prepared file to a print manager. A real person must inspect the printer and explicitly start the print.

What hardware and services would I need?

Development uses a Cardputer ADV and a connected Windows PC. Some optional online services may cost extra. The planned printing idea also needs a compatible printer and a person to approve starting a print.

Is there a Kickstarter date or price?

Not confirmed. A reliable physical prototype, safety testing, manufacturing quotes and realistic delivery costs must come before crowdfunding commitments. You can follow the source and ask for email updates below.
